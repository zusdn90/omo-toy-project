# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Solo food explorers and local content planners who want to compare neighborhood restaurants quickly, often on a mobile browser while deciding where to eat or what to review next. They need a fast map-first workflow that keeps the selected place, shortlist, review notes, and source context synchronized.

## Product Purpose

Omo is a personal MVP for turning local restaurant data into a usable exploration queue: neighborhood selection, Kakao/VisitKorea/Naver source-backed candidates, a synchronized map, an automatic Top 5, and lightweight personal reviews. Success means the user can choose a neighborhood or saved list, understand why places are surfaced, open details, and record their own impressions without losing map context.

## Brand Personality

Calm, practical, and editorial. The interface should feel like a well-kept field notebook for food scouting: precise enough to trust, warm enough to invite exploration, never loud for its own sake.

## Anti-references

Do not look like a generic SaaS dashboard, a purple-gradient AI mockup, a dark crypto map, or a tourist brochure. Avoid gradient text, decorative glass cards, repeated identical card grids, low-contrast labels, and implementation jargon such as framework names or API plumbing in visible UI.

## Design Principles

1. Map and list are one instrument. Selection state must feel continuous across marker, card, and detail surfaces.
2. Source context matters. Kakao, VisitKorea, Naver, and seeded data should be visible without overwhelming the place itself.
3. Progressive detail beats modal sprawl. Keep the map and shortlist primary; reveal address, reasons, links, and reviews only when useful.
4. Editorial calm over dashboard noise. Use spacing, type weight, and restrained color to guide scanning.
5. Trust the data path. Empty, loading, and fallback states should explain what happened without exposing secrets or developer machinery.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, visible keyboard focus, 44px touch targets on mobile, no color-only source encoding, and motion that is subtle enough to respect reduced-motion preferences. Korean copy is primary, with concise labels and no decorative punctuation that harms scanning.
